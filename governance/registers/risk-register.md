# MarketingLabAI Risk Register

| ID | Category | Risk | Impact | Mitigation | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RISK-001 | Product access | Engineering and controlled synthetic design-partner rehearsal are authorized, but real-customer activation remains frozen. | Product quality can be rehearsed, but no market evidence is collected yet. | Complete browser, accessibility, recovery and failure rehearsal; require explicit founder approval before external invitations or real data. | Reduced - rehearsal authorized |
| RISK-002 | Architecture | Legacy CLI paths remain available outside the canonical application root. | A future caller could still select the wrong runtime path. | Require future interfaces to use CanonicalApplication; retire or migrate legacy paths deliberately. | Reduced — monitor |
| RISK-003 | Security | The API uses the authorized facade, but a live identity provider and operational secret handling are not configured. | Misconfigured deployment could prevent or weaken trusted authentication. | MLAI-027.6 must validate provider configuration, credential transport, and secrets. | Reduced - monitor |
| RISK-004 | Data integrity | Legacy JSON data may require migration while SQLite is canonical for the pilot. | Incomplete migration or duplicate records could confuse operators. | Keep JSON compatibility outside CanonicalApplication and define explicit migration before customer import. | Reduced — monitor |
| RISK-005 | Product quality | Approved Positioning and Strategy are integrated, but market validation remains incomplete. | Synthetic decisions may be mistaken for proven effectiveness or the full MIOS promise. | Preserve evidence and limitations; use the authorized synthetic rehearsal to improve the journey while keeping real-data activation frozen. | Reduced - monitor |
| RISK-006 | Operations | Single-instance Waitress, local rate limits, recovery, health, and runbooks are implemented but not rehearsed in a selected host. | Deployment-specific failure may remain. | Rehearse deployment and restore before reconsidering a customer pilot. | Reduced - monitor |
| RISK-007 | Privacy | Synthetic policy and safe logs exist; jurisdiction, customer terms, and subprocessors remain unapproved. | Customer data could be processed without complete legal controls. | Customer data stays prohibited until explicit privacy and founder approval. | Controlled - gate closed |
| RISK-008 | Commercial | Founding prices are not supported by unit economics or usage policy. | Unsustainable customer commitments. | Keep pricing hypothetical until cost and packaging review. | Deferred |
| RISK-009 | Product trust | Marketing Intelligence Score lacks a validated evidence model. | Misleading or gameable customer claims. | Keep future-only until explainability and validation requirements are met. | Deferred |
| RISK-010 | Strategy validity | A governed Strategy lifecycle exists before situation synthesis, market validation, measurement, and real outcomes. | Synthetic decisions could be mistaken for proven strategy. | Require approved positioning and verified evidence, expose unknowns, keep confidence non-predictive, and preserve the pilot freeze. | Controlled - monitor |
| RISK-011 | Environmental evidence | PESTLE inputs are user-supplied and may become stale or incomplete. | Strategy context may omit material external change. | Require source, timestamp, confidence, visible factor gaps, and no autonomous market claims. | Controlled - monitor |
| RISK-012 | Objective validity | Human-supplied targets may lack reliable baselines. | Targets could be mistaken for forecasts or commitments. | Require metric, method, timeframe, evidence linkage, visible gaps, and non-forecast limitations. | Controlled - monitor |
| RISK-013 | Marketing-mix coherence | A complete mix may be mistaken for a validated route to market. | Teams could over-trust untested pricing, channels, or measurement assumptions. | Require objective links, explicit constraints, human-supplied commercial inputs, visible gaps, and non-attribution limitations. | Controlled - monitor |
| RISK-014 | Design-partner activation | Free full-feature entitlement could be mistaken for approval to process real customer data. | Privacy, identity, support, or recovery controls could be bypassed. | Keep entitlement and activation separate, return activation unauthorized, expose all blockers, and require a new founder decision. | Controlled - gate closed |
| RISK-015 | Signup identity | Tenantless signup conflicts with the existing tenant-bound browser session. | A cosmetic form could be mistaken for usable onboarding. | Provide a pre-tenant authenticated claim API; require deployed identity and browser rehearsal in MLAI-030.2. | Controlled - gate closed |
| RISK-016 | External identity | The initial Entra selection was superseded before deployment after comparison with Google Cloud Identity Platform. | Contradictory configuration could cause the wrong provider to be deployed. | Preserve ADR-0021 as historical, make ADR-0022 authoritative, and validate the selected provider in configuration and continuity tests. | Controlled - superseded before deployment |
| RISK-017 | Google Cloud identity | Project, billing, redirect, key retrieval, or token-audience configuration may be incorrect in the first controlled Google Cloud project. | Login could fail, incur avoidable cost, or accept a token for the wrong project. | Pin issuer, project audience and RS256; require time and subject claims; enable budget alerts and audit logs; prohibit SMS and paid extensions; rehearse invalid-token and recovery cases before invitations. | Controlled - deployment rehearsal open |
| RISK-018 | Google sign-in availability | Browser sign-in depends on the hosted Google Identity Services client. | New login can be temporarily unavailable during a Google or network outage. | CSP permits only the Google client and Identity Toolkit endpoint; preserve local governed sessions and display a safe login error. | Open - rehearse |
| RISK-019 | Password signer exposure | The unused SCRYPT signer configuration appeared in an administrative setup transcript. | Future password hashes would face additional risk if password sign-in were enabled without remediation. | Keep password sign-in disabled with no password users; require security review and fresh credential treatment before enabling it. | Controlled - disabled |
| RISK-020 | Privacy acceptance | Synthetic acceptance could be mistaken for consent to process actual partner data. | Real customer, employee, supplier, transaction, or confidential data could enter the pilot prematurely. | Version and bind synthetic acceptance, deny real categories at runtime, keep real-data periods unset, and require a separate founder activation decision. | Controlled - gate closed |
| RISK-021 | Production identity evidence | Local controls could be mistaken for proof that deployed Google and hosting configuration is secure. | A founder could assess activation using incomplete or stale deployment evidence. | Require explicit boolean evidence for key restrictions, audience, domains, audit logging, invalid-token, revocation, and tenant-isolation rehearsals; missing evidence blocks readiness and never authorizes real data. | Controlled - evidence gate closed |
| RISK-022 | Operational evidence integrity | Unsupported, stale, or wrong-deployment claims could be mistaken for recovery and support readiness. | Activation assessment could rely on controls that were never rehearsed or no longer apply. | Persist immutable environment- and commit-bound expiring evidence; retain failures and require remediation before the unchanged check is rerun. | Controlled - evidence gate closed |
| RISK-023 | Partner acceptance integrity | Caller claims or one partner's result could be mistaken for acceptance by both tenants. | Tenant isolation or unresolved journey failures could be bypassed. | Bind acceptance to the authenticated approved tenant, current privacy acceptance, all prior readiness gates, and partner-specific immutable scenario evidence. | Controlled - acceptance gate closed |
| RISK-024 | Activation scope escalation | A narrow Velani pilot could be mistaken for general real-data or production approval. | Other tenants or prohibited categories could activate without evidence or consent. | Require append-only founder decisions bound to Velani, exact categories, deployment and expiry; preserve immediate suspension and default-deny every omitted activity. | Controlled - default deny |
| RISK-025 | Cloud persistence | Deploying the SQLite runtime to replaceable Cloud Run storage could lose tenant, session, privacy, readiness or activation evidence. | Governance state could disappear or diverge during restart, revision or instance replacement. | Continue to refuse Cloud Run with SQLite; use only the verified PostgreSQL boundary and require separate deployment and activation decisions. | Reduced - durable adapter evidenced; deployment frozen |
| RISK-026 | PostgreSQL evidence | Adapter and synthetic-copy tests could be mistaken for proof of live PostgreSQL migration and recovery. | A hosted cutover could expose SQL, sequence, isolation or restore failures that mocks cannot reveal. | Preserve the commit-bound local and Cloud migration, contract, backup, restore and cleanup manifest; rerun for a changed adapter, schema, target or real-data cutover. | Reduced - external synthetic evidence passed at b09055a |
| RISK-027 | Private deployment | A synthetic service could be mistaken for public or real-data activation, or broad IAM and mutable images could bypass its boundary. | Unauthorized access, cost escalation or premature customer processing could occur. | Require a dedicated runtime identity, digest-pinned image, no public invoker, exact secrets, one-instance ceiling, IAM denial and rollback evidence. | Controlled - external deployment evidence pending |
| RISK-028 | Deployment configuration drift | Manually reconstructed Cloud Run commands can diverge from tested variable names, factories, secret bindings, or bounds. | Failed revisions, avoidable cost, and misleading deployment evidence could recur. | Enforce the canonical manifest renderer, PowerShell 5.1 preflight, CI regression, and ADR-0033 Durable Remediation before any separately authorized rerun. | Controlled - durable gate implemented |
| RISK-029 | Release sequencing and operator error | Manual gate tracking can run valid checks out of order, reuse stale evidence, or obscure the next safe action. | A later deployment step could proceed on an incomplete or mismatched foundation. | Enforce ADR-0034's ordered catalog, prerequisite controller, unique external run, hash-chained events, explicit failure classification, and CI validation. | Controlled - automation implemented |
| RISK-030 | Treating mutable controller state or retrospective repair as release authority could admit an unverified artifact. | Critical | Controller authority is superseded by ADR-0035; legacy runs remain non-deployable; introduce signed in-toto evidence, RFC 3161 timestamps, independent verification and Binary Authorization progressively. | Open |
| RISK-031 | Fragmented release scripts, shell-specific behaviour and manual evidence transfer can cause duplicate or incorrect operator actions. | Critical | ADR-0039 provides one pinned Python control plane, deterministic plan-bound approval, atomic indexed evidence, process locking and idempotent resume. | Reduced — repository validation pending |
| RISK-032 | Cloud preflight assembled from ad hoc shell commands could leak secret values, accept drifted infrastructure or accidentally mutate cloud state. | Critical | ADR-0040 pins resources, allowlists exact read-only verbs, owns JSON capture, forbids secret payload access and tests rejection before subprocess execution. | Reduced — implementation validation pending |
| RISK-033 | Windows batch semantics or changed executor code could fail outside test coverage or reuse an approval issued for different code. | Critical | ADR-0041 tests the `.cmd` boundary, pins Cloud CLI context, runs a same-adapter doctor before journaling and binds every plan to executor provenance. | Reduced — external Windows validation pending |
| RISK-036 | Release executor authorization gap | Successful impersonation could be mistaken for sufficient release permissions. | Release operations could fail or broad project roles could be granted unnecessarily. | MLAI-031.16 separates identity bootstrap from evidence-bound least-privilege permission authorization and forbids service-account keys. | Open - permission profile pending |

## MLAI-031.7 private-ingress contract split

- Risk: public canonical ingress could bypass the intended load-balancer boundary.
- Control: exact-value validation, bootstrap cross-contract tests and CI enforcement.
- Status: corrected pending commit and fresh provenance chain.

- MLAI-031.13: Risk of future pilots inheriting bootstrap placeholder origins is controlled by the durable ORIGIN_RECONCILED transition and startup precondition.

- MLAI-031.14 controls release execution risk caused by fragile PowerShell/Python quoting and untested operator wrappers.

- MLAI-031.15 controls release failure risk caused by expiring browser-user Cloud SDK tokens during non-interactive execution.

| RISK-034 | Database provider drift | Treating Cloud SQL, AlloyDB or an alternative PostgreSQL provider as an interchangeable toggle could bypass Cloud Run connectivity, Secret Manager, backup/restore, cost, tenant-isolation and release-control evidence. | A provider change could silently invalidate the controlled hosted persistence boundary. | ADR-0048 locks Cloud SQL for PostgreSQL as the initial managed provider and requires separate evidence for AlloyDB or any alternative provider. | Controlled - governance locked |
| RISK-035 | Tenant isolation during provider migration | A future managed-provider migration could preserve schema shape but lose tenant isolation, membership, audit or activation semantics. | Cross-tenant data exposure or governance-state corruption could occur during scale migration. | Rerun tenant-isolation, migration parity, backup/restore and release-control evidence before any provider switch or real-data cutover. | Open - future migration gate |

| RISK-037 | Repository integrity | Legacy encoding corruption, stale generators or incomplete validation coverage could make a clean checkout appear healthy while infrastructure and governance contracts drift. | False-positive CI confidence, repeated operator repair loops and damaged decision records could undermine release safety. | ADR-0050 requires canonical read-only coherence checks, exact-hash legacy baselines, plan-bound repairs, governance identifier validation and explicit CI coverage. | Controlled - acknowledged debt under exact-hash retirement |
